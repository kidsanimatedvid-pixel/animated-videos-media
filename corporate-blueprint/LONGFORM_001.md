# Long-form #1 concept: "Blockbuster Didn't Lose to Netflix the Way You Think"

**Pillar:** rise and fall · **Target length:** 11–13 min (~1,700 words) · **Status:** concept. Research must be completed and verified before scripting.

## Why this one
- **Proven demand:** Blockbuster vs. Netflix is one of the most-watched business stories on YouTube. Company Man and others have covered it.
- **A clear originality angle:** almost every version tells the myth ("they laughed at a $50M deal and died"). The record is more dramatic. Blockbuster *was winning* in 2007 with Total Access, then an activist fight, a CEO exit, a strategy reversal and a debt load ended it.
- **Strong visual storytelling:** store maps, DVD mailers, a share-price line, a boardroom diagram, a debt "anchor".

## Structure
1. **Cold open (0:00–0:40):** 2007. Netflix's CEO tells investors Blockbuster's Total Access is hurting them [VERIFY exact quote and source]. "So how did the company that was winning end up with one store left?"
2. **Central question:** was Blockbuster killed by Netflix, or by itself?
3. **Background:** the store model. Late fees were about $800M in 2000 (CNBC 2020) [VERIFY against Blockbuster's own filings].
4. **The famous meeting (2000):** the $50M offer. What was actually said, according to sources (Randolph's book, Antioco's HBR piece) [VERIFY].
5. **Rising tension:** the late-fee removal and Total Access (2004–07). Subscriber numbers [VERIFY 10-K].
6. **The twist:** the Icahn proxy fight, Antioco's exit (2007) and the new CEO's reversal.
7. **Mechanism:** debt (the Viacom spin-off left about $1B) plus physical leases versus Netflix's asset-light mail and streaming model. An animated balance sheet.
8. **Consequences:** Chapter 11 in September 2010, the Dish purchase in 2011, the Bend, Oregon store.
9. **Conclusion:** strategies don't fail in a single meeting. They fail when a company stops funding the future to protect the present.

## Visual plan
- Blueprint floor plan of a 2004 store that "dissolves" into a mailing envelope.
- An animated 10-K page with the key numbers highlighted.
- A timeline rail across the bottom.
- Two boardroom-table diagrams: 2000 vs. 2007.
- Use original art throughout. No Blockbuster or Netflix logos (names in text only).
